Use the resources to make a decision reviewable

Start with one question that matters to your team. For a measurement exercise, identify the event that starts each clock and the condition that stops it. For a plan, identify who can approve a disruptive containment action. For a tabletop discussion, record what was known when the decision was made and what would cause you to reconsider it.

The calculator uses one incident per row, with three durations in minutes separated by semicolons: initial activity to detection; detection to containment; detection to recovery. It reports the last interval explicitly rather than assuming that every organisation uses MTTR with the same meaning. Small samples and uncertain start times require careful interpretation.

The plan generator produces a working draft, not an assessment of readiness. The tabletop scenarios are fictional conversations and do not execute attacks or test your infrastructure. A useful outcome is a specific improvement with an owner and a way to verify completion.

About the book

Confronto Cibernético, by Hiago Kin Levi and published by Brasport, discusses advanced persistent threats, incident response and coordination between defensive operations and threat intelligence. The edition described on this website is in Portuguese. Read the author biography in English or view the available editions.

Further study

For current external guidance, consult NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 and the CISA cybersecurity scenarios. These are independent references; the resources on this website are not endorsed or certified by those organisations.

You can link students and colleagues directly to any resource. The downloaded plan and exercise worksheets include permission for adaptation in internal or educational activities with attribution. The book, its cover and third-party material retain their own rights.
